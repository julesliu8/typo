Conceptualising love in Amharic: Lexis, metaphors, and cultural contexts
							| Studies in African Languages and Cultures Skip to main content Skip to main navigation menu Skip to site footer Open Menu Home About About the Journal Editorial Team Privacy Statement Current Issue All Issues Register & Submit Announcements Contact Search Register Login Home / Archives / Vol. 60 (2026) / Articles Conceptualising love in Amharic Lexis, metaphors, and cultural contexts Authors Aleksander Kacprzak Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznan DOI: https://doi.org/10.32690/SALC60.1 Keywords: concept of love, 											Amharic worldview, 											Amharic semantics, 											Ethiopian culture, 											ethnolinguistics Abstract Based on three sets of linguistic data and grounded in cognitive ethnolinguistic methodology, this article analyses the Amharic concept of love at three levels of granularity. First, it examines the difference between the two verbal exponents of the concept wäddädä ‘to love, like, want’ and afäqqärä ‘to love’, along with the specifics of the noun fəqər ‘love’, as well as the multitude of other categories formed from the two main roots fqr and wdd. Second, it discusses the basic metaphors of love, with particular focus on those unique to Amharic (e.g. fəqər lay mähon lit. ‘to be on love’, yäanǧät fəqər lit. ‘the intestine love’). Third, the article explores the most important subcategories of Amharic love, such as romantic love, family love, self-love and love of homeland. Overall, the article offers an in-depth account of the structure of the concept and demonstrates a range of links with fundamental beliefs and practices functioning in Ethiopian culture, such as the notion of an interventionist God, the central role of family relations, the tabooisation of sex, and a uniquely conceptualized relationship with one’s homeland. It also provides a strong argument for taking linguistic and cultural relativity into account in discussions of human experience and the conceptualization of love.
