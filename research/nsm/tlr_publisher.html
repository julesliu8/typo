About this journal

The current volume of The Linguistic Review is open access via Subscribe to Open (S2O). All accepted articles will be published under a Creative Commons license at no cost to authors. Learn more about De Gruyter Brill’s Subscribe to Open program.

Objective
The Linguistic Review publishes high-quality papers in phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics and pragma tics, within a framework of Generative Grammar and related disciplines, as well as critical discussions of theoretical linguistics as a branch of cognitive psychology.

Striving to be a platform for discussion, The Linguistic Review does not accept brief book reviews but we welcome review articles of im portant new monographs that represent new trends in the field.

The Linguistic Review is a peer-reviewed journal of international scope.

      Clause size, negation and aspect in Polish and Serbian subjunctives
      November 6, 2026
      Alberto Frasson, Dorota Klimek-Jankowska
      Download PDF
      This paper investigates the interaction between negation and aspect in Slavic subjunctive complements, focusing on a systematic contrast between Polish żeby -clauses and Serbian da -clauses. While both languages freely allow perfective aspect in affirmative contexts, a sharp asymmetry emerges under negation: Polish strongly disfavors the perfective, preferring the imperfective, whereas Serbian allows both aspects without restriction. Building on the Implicational Complementation Hierarchy and Exfoliation Theory, we argue that this contrast is a structural consequence of clause size. Polish żeby -subjunctives are analyzed as complements that lack a TP layer, forcing negation to merge low within the verbal domain. This low negation interacts directly with event structure, creating a semantic incompatibility with the bounded nature of perfective aspect. Conversely, Serbian da -clauses exhibit properties of a full TP, allowing negation to merge high in the inflectional domain. In this higher position, negation takes scope over the entire proposition, leaving the internal aspectual structure intact. An acceptability judgment task supports these claims, showing a robust penalty for perfective morphology in Polish that is absent in Serbian. Ultimately, the study proposes that aspectual behavior under negation serves as a novel diagnostic for clausal defectivity and structural height in Slavic complementation.

      On languages in the tactile modality
      October 9, 2026
      Carlo Geraci
      Download PDF
      While linguistic research on sign languages is getting larger and larger impact in the linguistic community, much less is known about languages in the tactile modality, namely the languages used by DeafBlind signers. These languages are originally adapted from their visual counterpart, although after that, they witness independent evolution. In this work, we present a detailed overview of the properties of tactile languages showing their degree of autonomy from visual sign languages and how these properties allow to re-assess some foundational aspects of human language, including the role of native users in driving linguistic change, and the impact of iconicity in transmitting meaning. The evidence discussed in the paper will give the tools to provide an informed answer to the question whether languages in the tactile-gestural modality should be considered as an autonomous externalization of human language.

      A palatalizer uncovers root sizes of Ukrainian verbs
      September 1, 2026
      Nataliya Shvets
      Download PDF
      This paper investigates the interaction between roots and suffixes in the present tense of Ukrainian verbs. Using Nanosyntax, we propose an analysis based on root size to show how different roots select the correct class markers, giving rise to two distinct conjugation classes. Our analysis also accounts for the 1sg, where the class markers are absent. We show that, despite their absence, the two classes remain distinguishable through the presence versus absence of an additional morpheme, a palatalizer. The palatalizer is identified by observing systematic root alternations in one of the two classes. We decompose palatalization into ingredients and examine how they are stored in the lexicon, showing that one of the two palatalizers is an independent morpheme. The discovery of this additional morpheme helps uncover the root sizes of the two classes.

      Thematic interpretations of intermediate external arguments are post-syntactic derivatives: lessons from causee
      August 20, 2026
      Zhuosi Luo
      Download PDF
      External arguments have long been argued to hold a special status in the literature on argument structure. Recent works have converged on the view that their thematic interpretations are post-syntactic derivatives. However, most of these studies focus exclusively on arguments serving as the highest arguments within certain domains, leaving the thematic interpretations of intermediate external arguments understudied. This study aims to address this research gap by exploring the different causee interpretations in the Teochew mue -causative and kə -causative. I demonstrate that, like other external arguments, intermediate ones also have their thematic interpretations contextualized through syntactically-oriented event structural interpretation as post-syntactic derivatives. However, intermediate external arguments, such as causees, require a more complex two-step contextualization mechanism. In this mechanism, the complement-oriented approach, widely adopted to analyze the highest external arguments, provides only the initial interpretation, which is further modified during subsequent syntactico-semantic derivations.

      Arabic morphology: from morphome to morpheme
      July 28, 2026
      Maris Camilleri, Ali Idrissi
      Download PDF
      Morphological exponence in Arabic verbal morphology deploys at least three concurrent realizational means: affixation, prosodic templatic variation, and stem-vowel alternation or ablaut). In this paper, we focus on stem-vowel alternation as a morphological exponent in Arabic verbal paradigms, mainly in the second stem-vowel (V2). Traditional accounts have associated V2 alternation mainly with aspect and voice . We show that such accounts fail to capture the full picture. Adopting a comparative dialectal perspective, we demonstrate that V2 ablaut displays two distinct grammatical profiles in the Arabic dialect continuum. In Standard Arabic and many Eastern dialects, the distribution of V2 alternation is best analyzed as morphomic , since it does not consistently map onto a single morphosyntactic or morphosemantic class across derivationally related verbal measures. In Western dialects, by contrast, this same alternation, at least across the set of V-final verbs (defective), can be analyzed as morphemic, encoding a valency contrast between transitive and intransitive stems. Our analysis further reveals a systematic East–West divide in Arabic verbal morphology. More generally, we argue that Arabic provides a striking case of distributed exponence, in which V2 alternation overlaps functionally both with templatic structure and affixation in the realization of aspect, voice , and valency . Additionally, by tracing the diachronic shift in Arabic verbal morphology from morphomic patterning to morphemic interpretation, we aim to shed light on how morphological systems may evolve toward grammatical transparency.

      Old markers, new motivations: phonological and non-phonological alternations in a variety of Arabic from Aden (Yemen)
      April 17, 2026
      Ori Shachmon, Noam Faust
      Download PDF
      The variety of Arabic spoken by the Jews of Aden (Yemen) exhibits three noteworthy characteristics, all remarkable and distinctive in the Arabic dialectal space. First, it expresses its default final stress as duration, in a way that blurs the difference between phonemically long and short vowels under stress. The difference is nevertheless revealed as stress shifts away when a suffix is added. Second, older stem vocalizations are preserved for some verbs, yet only upon the addition of object suffixes. Synchronically, these cases illustrate non-optimizing allomorphy, in which a form with an object suffix is not necessarily based on the same form without that suffix. Finally, as in Old Arabic, the 3rd masculine singular perfect form has the suffix /-a/, yet its distribution is exclusive to two environments: (i) before /h/-initial object suffixes, and (ii) word-finally after geminate verbs of measure I. The motivation for the emergence of final /-a/ in both cases is strictly phonological.

      Quotation as a challenge for feature-driven phases: An argument for Zwart’s merge
      November 24, 2018
      Jan Wiślicki
      Download PDF
      The present paper discusses the problem of deriving quotational expressions (QEs)–a relatively unexplored field in the generative tradition–in the context of phase theory (PT). QEs are taken to be built within Narrow Syntax. While this fact is empirically well-motivated, it turns out to be conceptually problematic for cyclic derivations driven by feature makeup. Empirical data show that QEs represent a unique mixture of conflicting properties. On the one hand, they must be interpretable as both atomic and not. This extends to two readings of the embedding of enquotation, which are argued to follow from recursive and purely iterative, non-recursive interpretation. On the other hand, having no limits in length and scope, QEs are more challenging for PT than other non-compositional chunks. I argue that these effects unearth an important conceptual contrast. While they pose certain problems for the Minimalist approach, they naturally follow from the account of Narrow Syntax proposed by Jan-Wouter Zwart. In this regard the gap in applicability of each account to the discussed phenomenon sheds new light on the role of features in PT.

    Submission process

      • Set your manuscript according to the guidelines below
      • Authors are asked to check their manuscripts very carefully befores submitting them in order to prevent delays and extra costs at proofing. Authors will receive PDF proofs for correction which must be returned by dates given in the publication schedule

    • Before submitting your article, please have a look at our AI policy guidelines.
    • Manuscripts of articles should be in English. Authors whose native language is not English are asked to have their article carefully checked by a native speaker
