Lastly, I will mention Morita's (1996: 258) comment about the differences between
these two words. He says that since the URESHII feeling is an individual one, one
could not say "URESHI TV bangumi" (a TV program which makes the audience feel
*URESHI), since it is not an individual but a broad audience who feel something good
about the TV program. On the other hand, one could say "TANOSHI TV bangum" (a
TV program which make the audience fel TANOSHI) since the use of the TANOSHI
feeling is not limited to an individual.? If there is a situation where the sentence implies
that an individual person feels happy because of a TV program, as seen in the following
sentence (26), one could use URESHIT.
Thus, the difference between URESHIl and TANOSHIl can be summed up by means
of the following contrasts:
1. While URESHI can be used only as a subjective adictive "(Watashi wa) [Y ga]
URESHI (I) feel URESHI (about XI)", TANOSHI can be used in two frames: the
subjective adjectival use frame "(Watashi wa) [Y ga) TANOSHI (I) feel TANOSHIL
[about XI)" and the attributive use frame "X wa TANOSHI (People in general feel) Y is
TANOSHI)".
