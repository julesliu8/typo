In this thesis, I will explore the world of emotions of Japanese people as reflected in
the Japanese lexicon/expression. As Leggett (1973: 170) suggests, Japanese people are
often misunderstood by non-Japanese people as being emotionles because of their
frequent suppression of emotions in certain situations. Japanese people, however, are
"intensely emotional people, and inner, subjective experiences -- felings - are extremely
important to them" (Matsumoto 1996: 127). Nakamura (1964; 531) also postulates that
the thinking of most Japanese tends to be intuitive and emotional, when he says that "the
expressive forms of Japanese sentences put more emphasis upon emotive factors than on
cognitive factors. The forms of expression of the Japanese language are more oriented to
sensitive and emotive nuances than directed toward logical exactness".
1.2. The Goals of the Study
In this thesis, we aim to examine the meaning and use of everyday Japanese emotion
words or expressions. By exploring the world of emotions through language, we will
try to throw light on some characteristis of Japanese people and their culture. We will
also try to describe the norms relating to both verbal and nonverbal Japanese emotional
expressions, using cultural scripts; c.g. what kinds of emotions Japanesc people are
expected to express or supress in certain situations. These cultural scripts concerning
emotions vary in different cultures. Grasping these norms is necessary for smooth cross-
cultural communication between Japanese and non-Japanese people.
This thesis has two separate and complementary goals. The first is to examine the
meanings of various Japanese terms for emotions, and to show how their meanings and
usages are relatid to the prevailing modes of thinking and culture of the Japanese people.
