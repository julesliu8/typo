4.1. Inflections and Derivations of Adjectives and Verbs
The Japanese language has two adjectival subclasses: -adjectives (keiyoshi) and
Na-adjectives (keiyoo-dooshi). Modification is the most common function of these
adjectives. While the non-past indicative form of I-adjctives can be used in
modification, Na-adjectives need to be followed by na" (the attributive form of the
copula "da" when they are used as modifiers:
Had (oloved Noun (person)

As Matsuda et al (1993: 5) states, I-adjectives inflect for tense (past and non-past) and
mood. Na-adjectives do not inflect for tense and mood by themselves. The part which
inflects is the copula (Backhouse 1984: 172; Kindaichi 1988 Vol.2: 133). As for verbs,
they inflect for tense and mood like I-adjectives. The following is the list of inflections of
