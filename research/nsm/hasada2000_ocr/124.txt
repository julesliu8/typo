7.1. URESHIT and TANOSHI
To begin with, the difference between the two adjectives URESHII and TANOSHI
will be discussed. While both of them are often translated as meaning happy' in
English, each word has distinctive connotations.
First of all, while URESHII can be used only as a subjective adjective "(Watashi wa)
[X ga) URESHII (1) feel URESHIT (about VI)", TANOSHIl can be used in two frames:
the subjective use frame "(Watashi wa) [Y ga) TANOSHI (I) feel TANOSHI [about
X)" and the attributive use frame "Y wa TANOSHI (People in general feel Y is
TANOSHI)".
The Attributive Use of TANOSHIT

TANOSHI here in example (1) and (2) is used as an attributive adjective. URESHI

that TANOSHI, but not URESHI, is an emotion word which is used for referring to

The Subjective Use of URESHI and TANOSHI
Secondly, we will examine the differences in the subjective use of URESHII and
TANOSHI. Hirose and Shooj (1994: 138-139) say that URESHIT is used "to express
the pleasure of being moved or excited when something good happens, or when things
go as the speaker wished them to. It implies a cherful and happy feling", while
TANOSHII "expresses a casual and light mood, void of unpleasantness or boredom.
Unlike URESHI, which suggests a reaction to a specific event, as in the example
Nyuusaku shiken ni katte reshit I'm URESHIT that I passed the school entrance
examination), TANOSHI describes the overall atmosphere".
