Grammar notes Basic clause structure and the transitive construction Mano is a tonal language with three basic tones, low, mid and high. The basic word order is S AUX (O) V X, where S is a subject, V is a verb, AUX is an auxiliary expressing grammatical semantics of tense, aspect, mode and polarity, indexing the person and number of the subject; some portmanteau forms also incorporate a 3SG pronoun whose underlying position is that of direct object, argument of postposition or inalienable possessor; O is a direct object (whose absence makes the verb intransitive, except some constructions with portmanteau auxiliaries), X is an oblique (indirect object or adjunct), most often represented by a postpositional phrase. Depending on the type of temporal, aspectual and modal construction, the verb can take a specific tonal form, to which suffixes may additionally be adjoined. Certain kinds of nominalization are also marked by grammatical tone. Complex predicates consisting of a nominal and a verbal part are common; depending on their semantic and syntactic properties they can be analyzed as more or less bound forms, see Khachaturyan (2017). The word order in the NP is N2 – N1 (N1 is the head noun, N2 is the dependent noun), N – Adj (the adjectival modifier follows the head noun). With certain dependent nouns the head nouns appear in their tonal construct form. Khachaturyan, Maria. 2017. Bound noun plus verb combinations in Mano. Mandenkan 57. 3–24. Case system There is no morphological case on nouns. Verb lemmas Lemmas are shown in the form of bare stems.
