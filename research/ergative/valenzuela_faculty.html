Biography

Pilar Valenzuela earned a B.A. in Hispanic Linguistics and Literature and a Diploma in Anthropology from the Pontificia Universidad Católica del Perú, as well as an M.A. and Ph.D. in Linguistics from the University of Oregon. Her doctoral research on Shipibo-Konibo was supported by a three-year fellowship from the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, Germany. Her dissertation, Transitivity in Shipibo-Konibo Grammar, received the Mary R. Haas Book Award from the Society for the Study of the Indigenous Languages of the Americas.

A specialist in the Indigenous languages of the Peruvian Amazon, Valenzuela’s research brings together linguistic description and documentation, functional-typological and historical-comparative linguistics, language contact, and Amazonian Spanish. She has conducted extensive fieldwork on several Indigenous languages of the region. Her scholarship has made significant contributions to the description and comparative study of Panoan and Kawapanan languages. Another major strand of her research examines the distinctive characteristics of Peruvian Amazonian Spanish and the effects of long-term contact among Spanish, Quechua, and Indigenous Amazonian languages.

Valenzuela’s research has received substantial external support, including two major grants from the National Science Foundation: a Documenting Endangered Languages grant for the documentation of Shiwilu and Shawi, and a second NSF grant focused on the documentation and computational analysis of Amahuaca.

At Chapman, Valenzuela held the prestigious Wang-Fradkin Professorship for Scholarly Excellence (2006–2008) and received the 2012 Global Engagement Research Award from Chapman University Research and Sponsored Programs Administration, as well as Faculty Excellence Awards for scholarly and creative activity in 2013 and 2017. Her international distinctions include appointments as Distinguished Visiting Professor and guest scientist at universities in Australia and the Netherlands. In Peru, she was appointed Extraordinary Visiting Professor at the Universidad Nacional Intercultural de la Amazonía (UNIA) in 2018.

Her publications include books and pedagogical works, numerous articles and book chapters on Amazonian languages, linguistic typology, language history, and Peruvian Amazonian Spanish, and co-edited volumes published by Oxford University Press and Brill.

A defining dimension of Valenzuela’s career is her long-term collaboration with Indigenous communities and educators in the Peruvian Amazon. For more than three decades, she has supported intercultural bilingual education, Indigenous teacher training, the development of pedagogical materials and writing systems, and community-based efforts to maintain and revitalize endangered languages. This work seeks to strengthen intergenerational language transmission, expand educational opportunities in Indigenous languages, and support the linguistic and cultural vitality of Amazonian communities.
